RC Time Attack devlog #1: feel before features

RC Time Attack is a small racing game I'm creating in Godot 4.7, somewhere between Trackmania and real RC racing. One lap of about thirty seconds, a standing start, and R restarts instantly. The goal is simple: after twenty minutes you know the track, you lap consistently, and you still want two more tenths.

One rule

Everything in the project answers to one sentence: never harder through complicated controls, only deeper through the physical consequences of simple inputs. Keyboard first, no drift button, no landing bonus, and no progression that changes lap times. It should be easy to keep on the track and hard to take to the limit.

Feel first

The biggest risk in a game like this is physics that isn't fun, so nothing else was allowed in until a single corner on a flat floor felt good. That took longer than expected. A fully emergent tyre model made drifting realistic and completely uncontrollable, so I replaced it with a readable layer: a quick stab of the brake while steering starts the slide, the steering key sets the nose angle, throttle holds it, and letting go ends it.

In the air the car follows the same idea. Gas lifts the nose, brake drops it, and steering tilts the roll. Jumps first felt unfair, so they gained a little arcade stability, while the controls stayed the same.

Setup as technique

I like how Trackmania keeps everyone on the same car, so tuning here is open to everyone and nothing is a straight upgrade. My first sliders only changed numbers, and that was boring. Now each slider has to change how you drive:

SliderOne endOther end
TyresSlide: a lift of the throttle starts a wide driftGrip: you need the brake, and corners get sharper
WeightFront: wins the corner entry, brakes shorterRear: wins the exit, carries speed through the drift
The balance rule: every advantage has a real cost. An excellent driver on the base setup should still be competitive.

The jumps out of nowhere

The track is built from modular pieces, like Trackmania blocks. At one point the car would suddenly hop on a flat straight. Instead of guessing, I added a log that records every sudden change in the car's motion. It showed the chassis catching the edge of the next piece at the joints: 20 hits in 360 crossings. Merging the whole track into one collision surface brought that to zero.

That became a rule for the future track editor: the pieces can be modular, but the collision has to be one surface.

What's next

A Trackmania-style track editor with pieces that snap together on a grid, more track pieces, and bots that learn to drive the track so I can test new layouts quickly. Sound comes later, on purpose: the car has to feel right without it first.
