Jigsaw: designing Act One for a Steam demo

Jigsaw is a first-person 3D jigsaw game in Godot 4.7. You solve real puzzles, up to 2,000 pieces, on a big table in a celestial atelier. The first versions were a lovely toy. The question for the Steam demo was how to make it a game without breaking the calm.

The Act One loop

Act One is the demo and the build target. Puzzles are grouped into acts. Playing earns Fits, and Fits buy upgrades. Objectives on each puzzle give stars. One rule above all: nothing in an act can be failed. You can only do it better.

The economy is tuned with a headless simulation rather than by guessing. The target: a typical run, one or two stars per puzzle, owns about 70 to 80% of the shop by the end of the demo. Full stars own nearly all of it. Prices change; earnings don't.

Charms, not consumables

The first version had consumable boosts on an upgrade tree. Playing it, I found it "not intuitive, and hard to understand what's going on", so it went. Now there are two kinds of upgrade:

Tools
A short tree, bought once and always on: sorting trays, the wall display, deeper pockets, tweezers.
Charms
Bought once, worn in a limited frame, a bit like Moonlighter. The charms are themselves jigsaw pieces.

The design rule for charms: a charm changes how you play, never what the answer is. Hints are rare and limited. Glowing pieces are not an upgrade.

Progress lives in the room

I didn't want progress in a menu. So the atelier is the hub: puzzles you haven't played wait as boxes in the wall niches, and finished ones hang framed. The upgrade tree is a constellation you open at the armillary sphere. After a puzzle, you can watch a replay of your whole build, piece by piece.

The atelier

This is the part where my 3D background does the work. The atelier is a modular room built in Blender from six reusable wall pieces across 18 bays, with a dome, arched doors and a navy-and-brass finish made in Substance Designer. The big 10 by 6 metre worktable is a deliberate choice: on a 2,000-piece puzzle, space to sort is part of the fun.

Status: I'm waiting on Steam for the store page. The Act One demo comes next.
